# Changelog

## v4.0.1

### New Fish

- **Red-bellied Piranha** — a 34 cm, 1 kg South American characin, the first piranha in the mod. It takes a rod or a large trap in warm fresh water (best from 20 °C up, biting through the day with peaks at dawn and dusk and almost nothing at night), and it shares the pike and muskellunge bait table: meat, fish and the lures a pike would take. Its model and skin are new. The body is deep and strongly compressed, with the blade-toothed lower jaw jutting out, and the skin is a photograph of a real red-bellied piranha carried onto the model through its own along-body frame: a dark slate back, silver flanks under heavy brassy spangling, the vivid orange-red throat, breast and belly, red at the base of the anal fin and a broad dark band across the tail. It has its own fillet — a lean white freshwater meat on one side, that skin on the other — is carried one-handed like a bass and hangs on the small fish mount. An existing `fish.json` gains its row and bait preferences when the mod updates

- **Southern Stingray** — a 0.9 m disc and 1.6 m overall, 26 kg, the first ray in the mod. A rod-only sea catch (best 17–31 °C, nocturnal, biting hardest at dusk and through the night) that takes the same bottom baits as the flounder and the cod, and can be speared in the shallows like a flounder. Its back is a photograph of a real southern stingray — a whole specimen, laid flat and shot from above — mapped straight onto the model, so the eyes, spiracles and the dull olive-brown of a landed ray are the animal's own. Carried two-handed, hangs on the medium fish mount, and fillets into skate wing: two to four pieces of lean white meat, on the catfish fillet model the mod's other lean bottom fish use
- **Ocellate River Stingray** — a 0.42 m disc and 0.79 m overall, 5 kg, the fresh-water ray. Rod or large trap in warm fresh water (best 20–32 °C, nocturnal), on the catfish and bottom-feeder bait table, and spearable where it lies half buried. Its skin is a photograph of a real ocellate river stingray mapped straight on: a dark brown disc covered in orange ocelli ringed with black, crowding into small dense spots at the margin. Carried one-handed, hangs on the medium mount, and gives one or two lean white fillets, on the same catfish fillet model
  Both rays are built from the CadNav stingray model Cole supplied, rebuilt at a usable resolution with the anatomy it had none of — eye bulges and spiracles, the mouth, nostrils, five pairs of gill slits and the cloaca underneath, pelvic fins, the fin folds along the tail and the barbed sting a third of the way down it — and each disc reshaped to its own species' outline, traced from that species' photograph. An existing `fish.json` gains both rows and their bait preferences when the mod updates

### New & Reworked Models

- **New mod logo** — a largemouth bass leaping out of a splash, traced from the mod's own Largemouth Bass model: white in a black frame in the mod list and blue when hovered, in the same style as Geb's other mods. The wiki's browser-tab icon is the bass too
- **The Cherry Salmon looks like a cherry salmon** — its skin was the Steelhead Trout's, so the fish and especially its fillet were hard to tell apart from a steelhead. Both are now painted from a photograph of a real yamame: an olive back with black spots, the row of dark oval parr marks down the flank, a rose stripe along the lateral line and a creamy belly, with olive fins above and orange-washed ones below. The fillet carries that skin on one side and a paler coral flesh on the other, lighter and less red than the steelhead's it used to copy
- **The trout fillets are their own fish** — the skin side of every trout fillet in the mod was vanilla's Steelhead Trout fillet texture under a colour shift. One photograph of one fish, recoloured five ways: the brook, brown, cutthroat, lake and rainbow fillets all tracked Bohemia's lightness at 0.998 or better across the whole skin, and the Rainbow Trout's was not even recoloured — 98% of it was identical pixel for pixel. Since steelhead and the mod's trout are caught from the same ponds and share one catch category, a player could hold several fillets and see one picture with different names on it. All five are now painted from the fish's own body, carried across through the model's along-body frame, so the fillet shows the markings of the catch it came from:
  - **Brook Trout** — the pale worm-track vermiculations over the back, yellow spots down the flank, red spots in their blue haloes, and the orange belly along the cut edge. It is a char, and now looks like one
  - **Brown Trout** — the buttery gold flank with big black spots in pale haloes and orange-red spots along the lateral line, instead of a steelhead's unbroken stripe
  - **Rainbow Trout** — an olive back under heavy black spotting with the rose stripe along the lateral line and a silvery belly, painted from a photograph of a real freshwater rainbow trout and graded onto the mod's own live Rainbow Trout
  - **Cutthroat Trout** — an olive-gold flank under black spotting that crowds towards the tail, the rose line along the lateral line and a cream belly, carried over from the mod's own live Cutthroat Trout (its red throat slash sits forward of where a fillet is cut)
  - **Lake Trout** — a dark grey-green back and flank under the pale cream spots a lake trout is covered in, fading to a white belly, instead of a steelhead's pink stripe
  A steelhead *is* a sea-run rainbow trout, so vanilla's fillet and the Rainbow Trout's are still cousins: they are simply no longer the same image
- **Every fillet's meat is its own colour** — the eight fillets that use vanilla's steelhead fillet model all carried the same flesh photograph, and six of them carried it at almost the same colour: the Rainbow and Cutthroat Trout were 0.7 of 255 apart, which no eye can separate, with the Cherry Salmon sitting on top of both. The six pale ones now run deepest to palest in the order real fish do — Cherry Salmon, Brook Trout (a char feeding on crustaceans, the most orange of the trout), Rainbow, Cutthroat, Brown, then Lake Trout, which is nearly white — with the Sockeye's deep red and the Chinook's orange-red unchanged above them. No two of the eight are now closer than 5.5 in Lab, where the closest pair used to be 0.7. The picture went with the colour: each one is now painted from its own stretch of its own photograph of real raw fish — Bohemia's steelhead trout and walleye pollock fillets, which is where the mod's fillet meat came from in the first place — laid along the fillet through the model's own shape, and chosen for what that species' flesh really is: broad flakes and bold fat lines for the Chinook, finer and converging for the Cherry Salmon, and the pale, finely ribbed pollock for the Brown and Lake Trout, which are the two that are cream rather than salmon pink. Any two of the eight used to be the same photograph, matching at 0.996 to 0.998 in lightness; the closest pair now matches at 0.28. The cut edges follow the meat, and the skin sides are untouched

A classic for crappie and the other panfish (2.3x), trout and salmon (2x) and small sea fish such as mackerel (1.9x), good for walleye (1.8x), little use for catfish and carp. ### Balance

### Fixed

- **Runs alongside Zen's Sleeping Mod again** — the server stopped at "Can't compile "Game" script module!" with `Variable name 'GeneralConfig' already used as type name` (`zensleepconfig.c`, line 118). DayZ 1.30 refuses a variable named like any type it can see, in any loaded mod, and gebsfish's config classes had plain names (`GeneralConfig`, `FishConfig`, `BaitConfig`, `JunkConfig` and 25 more). All 29 now start with `Geb` (`GebGeneralConfig` and so on), so no other mod's variable can collide with them. The config files are unchanged: their keys are field names, not class names
- **Fish no longer show through the water** — 21 fish whose skins have see-through fin edges (the alligator gar, Asian seabass, bluegill, bowfin, brook, lake, rainbow and cutthroat trout, humphead wrasse, king crab, largemouth, smallmouth, spotted, striped, white and Neosho bass, yellow perch, redbreast sunfish, sockeye salmon, Atlantic sailfish and walleye) were drawn after the water surface, so in or under water they looked crisp and bright on top of it instead of tinted like every other fish. Their materials now cut the fins out cleanly, the way vanilla does for hair and fur, so they're drawn with everything else. The fins keep their shapes, and the white or dark rims the skins' photo backgrounds left along fin edges are gone
- **Lures fill their inventory slot** — every lure's picture was framed for a box several times bigger than the lure and seen from its head end, so it came out small and foreshortened. All seven lure models (the in-line spinners, the popper, both crankbaits, the squarebill, the spoons and the curly tail jigs) now show broadside, turned diagonally with the head up and to the right, and fill the slot. Their loot boxes now fit the lures, so a dropped lure no longer hovers above the ground, and lures lie on their side when dropped instead of standing on end

## v4.0.0

### Requirements & Compatibility

- DayZ 1.30 compatibility. This version no longer loads on 1.29
- DayZ 1.30: the mod's three script folders are listed with forward slashes in `config.cpp`. 1.30 Experimental silently skips a script folder written with backslashes, so none of the mod's scripts loaded there (no fishing, nets, bug digging, rod repair, fish recipes or config files) while its items still existed; forward slashes load on 1.29 and 1.30 alike
- DayZ 1.30: the mod compiles there. 1.30 removed the field holding an item's seconds since its last Central Economy update (`m_ElapsedSinceLastUpdate`) and never calls `OnCEUpdate`, which live bait aging and cooler chilling ran on, so the World scripts failed to compile and the server would not start. Both now run on 1.30's `OnCEIterate`
- DayZ 1.30: spear fishing checks the water with the game's new `CCTLiquid` condition, as the rod does, instead of the obsolete `CCTWaterSurfaceEx`, and refuses wells and pumps the way the rod does. Reach (3 m) and the depth limit are unchanged, and the check still runs once at the start of a stab
- DayZ 1.30: logs older than 3 days are still deleted, and the pre-3.3 file sweep still finds its files. 1.30's `FindFile` reads its last argument as the file system to search (Bohemia's DZEXP-134, not fixed before the 1.30 release), so `FindFileFlags.ALL` listed the server's own folder instead of `$profile:`; the mod now passes -1, which searches the normal way. The logger also keeps the names of its logs in `Gebs/logs/sessions.txt` and prunes from that list too, so cleanup keeps working if the -1 stops working once Bohemia fixes `FindFile`
- DayZ 1.30: the cast's weather fields are now `RainLevel` and `SnowLevel`. 1.30's script compiler refuses a variable with the same name as a type it can see, and vanilla has a type called `Rain`, so the old `Rain` field stopped the scripts compiling
- **Community Framework (CF) is now a required dependency** — the RPC system migrated to CF's RPCManager
- `DZ_Weapons_Melee` added as a required addon; all fish now have melee functionality (matching vanilla)
- New map yield support: Deadfall (credits: DapperDan)
- **Custom maps keep the mod's fish** — a map without a hook in the mod whose world data clears the game's catch list after the mod has registered wiped every gebsfish catch. Once the mission has built its world data (a dedicated server builds it twice), the mod now registers its catches again, ahead of the map's own: where both define a catch, `fish.json` and `junk.json` decide, and the map's extra catches stay. Third-party map fixes that fire the yield invoker again work too
- **The four "Fun" tackle boxes are removed** — `geb_FunGreenTackle`, `geb_FunPurpleTackle`, `geb_FunRedTackle` and `geb_FunYellowTackle` were the Green, Purple, Red and Yellow Tackle Box under a second class name (same box, same texture). Boxes already on a server keep loading with everything in them when they sit inside something (a tent, a container, a vehicle, a jon boat deck, an inventory): the old names stay as hidden aliases of their twins until the next wipe (never spawned or listed; repaired with duct tape or epoxy as before). One lying loose on the ground loads once more and is gone after the second restart, with its contents, so move them into something before updating. The mod's generated types.xml and cfgspawnabletypes.xml no longer list them (the world's loot has four tackle box classes fewer; the Yellow Tackle Box now spawns with lures like the other large boxes, which only its Fun twin used to), and a mission or trader list that copied them should take them out (the server logs them as not spawnable)
- **The Wooden Fish Mount is now the Small Fish Mount** — 3.3.2's mount keeps its class (`geb_WoodenFishMount`), so placed mounts and their trophies stay, as the new small plaque. A trophy too big for it (a pike, catfish, crab, shark, marlin or another catch that now needs the medium or large board) can be taken off it and hung on a bigger board, where it is permanent again
- **The bonito's classes are renamed** — `geb_Bonita` and `geb_BonitaFilletMeat` are now `geb_PacificBonito` and `geb_PacificBonitoFilletMeat` (the fish is the Pacific Bonito everywhere). Bonitos already on a server keep loading under the old name, as a hidden alias of the new class until the next wipe, when they are in an inventory, a container, a cooler or on a mount: they look, carry, cook, cool, mount and fillet as the Pacific Bonito. One lying loose on the ground is gone after the second restart. ### New Systems

- **Weather catch buffs** — global rain / storm / time-of-day multipliers in `WeatherSettings`, plus per-fish Rain/Storm/Dawn/Day/Dusk/Night multipliers on every species (trout favour rain, walleye / pike / catfish favour night, sturgeon favours storms). A stacked-multiplier cap prevents storm + night from compounding past 2.0x. Snowfall counts as rain (the heavier of the two picks the band), so the buffs also work on Sakhal, which snows instead of raining. Fishing rod description hints at the mechanic
- **Moon phase** — accurate synodic phase (Meeus Julian Date algorithm) computed from the in-game date drives a night-only catch buff: full-moon nights up to +20%, new-moon nights down to -10%. Independently toggleable
- **Water temperature** (ships switched off: set `TemperatureEffectEnable` to 1 in `general.json` to use it) — per-fish `TempOptimal` / `TempMin` / `TempMax` (degrees C) form a bell curve seeded from ecological buckets (cold deepwater, cold, cool, warm, tropical). The water temperature is the map's own from the game's world data (Chernarus 15 °C fresh / 23 °C sea, Livonia 20 / 25, Sakhal 2 / -0.5) with a seasonal swing (±6 °C fresh, ±3 °C sea, warmest in early August), so Sakhal fishes like an arctic map out of the box; a global `WaterTempOffset` shifts the whole curve for custom maps or themed servers (frozen lake `-10`, tropical `+5`) without editing every fish. **If you followed the earlier advice to set `WaterTempOffset` to `-5` for Sakhal, set it back to `0`:** the map's own cold water is now built in, and the old offset would push Sakhal 5 °C colder still
- **Bite-speed cycle scaling** — every fish carries a 24-hour `BiteSpeed` curve tuned to its real-world circadian pattern; the catching system aggregates the active pool (weighted by abundance and time of day) to drive how long you wait between bites
- **Bait / lure preference matrix** — every bait and lure carries per-fish multipliers that bias which fish takes the hook: worms and insects favour panfish and trout, soft-plastic worms favour bass, live minnow and salamander favour pike / musky / walleye, live shrimp favours reef / tropical fish, bitterlings favour pike / musky / walleye / bass, sardines and juvenile squid favour the big saltwater fish, in-line spinners favour bass / pike / musky, spoons favour trout and salmon, jigs favour bass / walleye. Numbered lure variants share one family entry (`geb_SpinnerBait` covers `geb_SpinnerBait1-4`; an exact numbered entry still overrides). Roughly 870 seeded pairings, all overridable in JSON, master `Enable` toggle at the top of `bait.json`. Final pick weight = CatchProbability x min(weather x time-of-day x water temperature, MaxStackedMultiplier) x bait
- **Hook-from-fish recovery** — filleting a fish has a small configurable chance (default ~1/250) to recover a damaged hook or lure "stuck in the fish", which drops on the ground with the fillets; the hook pool, weights, and health range are admin-tunable
- **Ultra-rare treasure catches** — an extremely rare chance on a successful catch to pull up a container full of loot. Two independent admin-defined pools: `TreasureContainers` decides what the treasure arrives as (weight, health range, and its own item count), `TreasureLoot` decides what goes inside (weight, health range, quantity range for stackables). Every item slot rolls the loot pool separately, so no two hauls match even from the same container. A gebsfish container listed there (a tackle box, cooler or bucket) only gets loot it would take. Default chance is `0.0002` — roughly **1 in 5000 catches**, not casts, so a session landing 60 fish has about a 1.2% chance of seeing one. Treasure only bites on a **proper fishing rod** — the crafted improvised rod never rolls (`RequireRealRod`, on by default) — and winching a haul up is brutal on tackle: each pull costs the rod a third of its maximum health, ruining a pristine rod in **three treasures** (`RodCatchesToRuin`, `0` disables the wear). Seeded with plain vanilla classnames so it works out of the box, and meant to be replaced. Toggle with `TreasureSettings.Enable`, or set `Chance` to 0
- **Spear fishing** — hold an Improvised Spear (bone- or stone-tipped) and stab into shallow water (up to 1 m deep, within 3 m): no bait, a quick action, and the fish of the shallows (carp, panfish, bass, bowfin and bullfrogs in fresh water; flounder, grunts and blue tang at sea; mullet and gar in both). A new `SpearFishingSettings` section in `general.json` holds an on/off switch, the find chance, the maximum depth, a predator chance and a weighted catch table with water types; the config editor edits it. A stab catches about one time in 7 (a fish every 40 seconds or so), and each stab wears the spear like vanilla's worm digging wears a hoe: Worn after about 10 stabs, ruined after about 34. Like a rod, which rolls for a predator once per cast, the spear rolls once per fish it lands (1% by default)
- **Cooler freezer system** — coolers keep contents cold, live bait is perishable, and a frozen fish can't be filleted until it thaws
- **Bait freshness rules** — each container has one job, and they do not overlap:
    * **Worm Container / Bug Catcher** — hold worms, grubs and insects, and pause the ~90 minute live-bait death timer
    * **Cooler** — pauses both food rot and the live-bait timer for anything inside
    * **Bait Bucket** — holds small aquatic catches only (minnows, bitterlings, sardines, juvenile squid, crayfish, shrimp, frogs, salamanders, clams, mussels, snails, starfish and jellyfish) and stops them rotting, which keeps the minnow, bitterling, sardine, juvenile squid, salamander and shrimp hook baits fresh. It does not accept worms or insects, so it is no help with their ~90 minute clock
    * **Water barrel** — a vanilla barrel at least half full of water (fresh, clean or salt) is a live well: whole fish, minnows and juvenile squid sitting in it don't rot. It doesn't keep worms or insects alive
    * **Tackle boxes** — can carry bait for convenience but preserve nothing
    * A ruined container preserves nothing: a ruined cooler stops chilling, and a ruined Worm Container, Bug Catcher, Bait Bucket or water barrel stops keeping anything fresh
    * Artificial baits never spoil - they have no freshness timer. The rubber worm sits in the bait slot, so a bite takes it like any bait; the hard lures (spinners, spoons, cranks, jigs) are hooks: they take hook wear on every catch and can be lost like any hook
- **Predator spawn rework** — three independent gates (per-action chance, weighted predator pick, per-predator min/max count) with separate chance values for fishing, filleting, failed casts, and net use. Land-only spawn search (no underwater wolves), optional warning sound RPC, configurable chat warning
- **Per-action config sections** — `BambooFishingNetSettings`, `DigBugsSettings`, `DigWormsSettings`, each owning its `FindChance` (per-attempt probability of finding anything) and a weighted `Catches[]` table. Tool wear still applies on a miss
- **Configurable net catches** — net spawn table entries carry an `Environment` field (1 pond, 2 sea, 3 both) for per-environment filtering; catches spawn into the net's cargo (4x4) first, falling back to the player's feet when full. By default the net brings up minnows, bullfrogs, salamanders and the seven crayfish in fresh water, and shrimp and the four juvenile bait squid at sea, so a net cast into the sea is no longer always empty; existing `general.json` files gain the new rows on the version bump, and the net's cargo takes them all. New bamboo net repair recipe: one Netting + a damaged net restores it to Worn
- **Fishing effects** — filleting leaves blood drops on the ground; flies swarm over rotten fish and fillets lying on the ground; and a cooler is always chilly: wherever it stands (on the ground, a boat's deck or the locker) it breathes a cold mist from its top every few seconds. Bites keep vanilla's own splash. All built from vanilla's particles

### Config Overhaul

- `fishingsettings.json` replaced by four self-documenting files in `$profile:Gebs/`: `general.json`, `bait.json`, `junk.json`, `fish.json`. **Clean break — no migration**: servers regenerate fresh defaults and re-apply their custom tuning by hand
- Fish are fully data-driven: one `Species` row per fish (classname, recipe shape, fillet/caviar/lobster results, meat counts, environment, catch method, catch probability, weather multipliers, temperature preference, BiteSpeed curve) feeds a single generic yield + fillet-recipe pipeline
- Master enable toggles for every catch-modifying system — disabled systems keep their tuned JSON values with no in-game effect
- Bait multipliers are written rounded to the nearest 0.01, so the file shows clean values instead of float noise
- Missing config sections re-seed with fully populated defaults; all four files carry a `ConfigVersion` stamp for future migrations
- Detailed admin-facing documentation strings on every field, kept under the engine's JSON string-length crash ceiling
- **How the config updates itself** — worth reading once before you tune anything, because it decides which of your edits survive a mod update:
    * Each of the four files carries its own `ConfigVersion`. On startup a file is only rewritten if something actually changed: a fresh generation, a section that had to be re-seeded, or a version bump. An up-to-date file is left completely alone — no rewrite, no modified timestamp
    * **A version bump rewrites all four files, not just the one that changed.** The stamp is per-file and every file compares it, so bumping the mod version touches `general`, `bait`, `junk` and `fish` even when only one of them gained anything. Your values are carried through untouched — the file is re-serialized from what was loaded, not regenerated from defaults
    * **Your existing values are never overwritten.** The update is strictly additive: default entries missing from a list get inserted, entries already there are left exactly as you set them. The one exception is the help text: every `...Info` line is documentation, never a setting, so each start puts the current text in (in `general.json`, `bait.json`, `fish.json` and `junk.json`), and an older file no longer keeps blank or outdated explanations; a file is only rewritten when a text actually changed
    * **Deleting an entry is not how you disable it.** A row you remove is treated as missing and gets re-added on the next version bump. To retire something permanently set its weight / chance / `CatchProbability` to `0` — that survives every update. A whole section emptied on purpose is respected, except `HookFromFishCatches`, `TreasureContainers` and `TreasureLoot`, which refill with the defaults when empty (set their weights to `0` instead); a section or list that is entirely absent from the file (or `null`) gets re-seeded, and so does a net, spear or dig section's missing `Catches` table
    * **New on/off toggles are re-applied during migration.** The engine's JSON loader builds each section of your file without its built-in defaults, so a field your file doesn't mention loads as `0`, and a toggle introduced in a later version would otherwise arrive switched off on every existing server. On startup the mod checks whether your file contains each newer toggle (`CraftFishMount`, `CraftDryingRack`, `RequireRealRod`, `RodCatchesToRuin`): a missing one gets its intended default, and once it is in your file the value you set, including `0`, is kept through every future update
    * Pre-3.3 layout files (`fishingsettings.json`, `Fish/Logs/`, `extras/mpmissions/`) are swept into `$profile:Gebs/gebs_oldfiles/` on first start so old and new never sit mixed together, and the emptied folders are removed afterwards. Nothing from the old monolithic config is migrated into the new files — it is archived so you can still read your old tuning
- **Rows the server can't use are named at startup.** A catch row added by hand to the net or spear table without a valid `Environment` (1 pond, 2 sea, 3 both), a treasure container with `MaxItems` 0, health levels out of order, a predator with `MaxCount` 0, and a bait row or preference with no classname each get a log line with the file, the row number, the classname and what the row will do. Nothing is changed in your files. A skipped `fish.json` species is now named as it sits in the file, with its classname and the reason (the row number used to drift after the first skip)
- Config editor: a row it can't draw (an empty row, or a fish, bait or preference without a classname) no longer blanks the tab; it's listed with a Remove button and kept in the file, and anything else that fails to draw says so instead of leaving a blank page. Classnames the mod doesn't ship, like the renamed `geb_Bonita`, are flagged, and What will bite? handles them as the server does. The fish weather, temperature, catch-chance and bite-rhythm fields, the bait multipliers and every table have help buttons
- **Config editor** — `docs/config-editor.html` (the wiki's 🛠️ Config Editor link) opens your four JSON files in the browser, edits them with sliders, toggles and tables, and downloads them again; nothing is uploaded anywhere. Its **What will bite?** tab shows the odds of every species for a map, date, hour, rain, water and bait, computed from your own files with the same maths the server runs, plus the junk share, the bite timing, the moon and the treasure odds. A field missing from a section or row counts as 0 there, as it does in the game; only a section missing altogether gets the defaults
