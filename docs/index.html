How the preference system works

When you cast, the mod builds a weighted pool of every fish eligible for your water and method. Each fish's weight is CatchProbability × min(weather × time-of-day × temperature, cap) × bait (the cap is MaxStackedMultiplier; see the odds guide) — so a worm at dawn in a warm pond makes bluegill dramatically more likely than a shark. Unlisted bait/fish pairs default to a neutral ×1.0, and the whole system can be disabled with Enable: 0 in bait.json without losing your tuning.

Systems

Every system is independently toggleable in the $profile:Gebs/ config files. Disabled systems keep their tuned values in JSON with no in-game effect.

🌦 Weather & Time of Day

Per-species Rain/Storm/Dawn/Day/Dusk/Night multipliers. Bass fire up at dawn and dusk, walleye and catfish wake at night, trout chase the rain. Stacked buffs are capped so a stormy night never runs away.

🌕 Moon Phases

Real synodic moon-phase math (Meeus algorithm) from the in-game date. Full-moon nights bite up to +20%, new-moon nights down to -10%. Night-only, independently toggleable.

🌡 Water Temperature

Every species has an optimal temperature and a survivable range, and the water follows the map and the season. Bass own warm summer lakes; trout and salmon take over in cold water, and on icy Sakhal the summer lakes belong to sculpin and trout and the sea to pollock and cod. A global WaterTempOffset adapts the whole curve to custom or themed maps.

🕐 Bite-Speed Cycles

Each fish has a 24-hour circadian activity curve. The active pool's aggregate drives how long you wait between bites — night fishing for catfish feels different from noon panfishing.

🪱 Living Bait

Worms, grubs, crickets and grasshoppers die about 90 minutes after you find them. Only a Worm Container, a Bug Catcher or a cooler pauses that clock — tackle boxes and the Bait Bucket do not. Artificial lures never die.

🧊 Cooler & Freezer

Coolers actively chill cargo to -5°C: rot stops, food freezes. Frozen fish can't be filleted until thawed.

🐺 Predators

Fishing, filleting, failed casts, net use and spear fishing can each draw wolves or bears (~1% per action by default, per-action configurable). Land-only spawns, optional warning sound for everyone nearby, and a chat warning for whoever caused it.

🪝 Hook Recovery

Roughly 1 in 250 fillets recovers a damaged hook or lure "stuck in the fish." Pool and damage range are configurable.

🥫 Junk Catches

Rods can pull configurable junk — wellies, pots, anything you add — about one catch in ten on every map and in every season by default, with weighted chances between items and spawn-damage ranges. Nets and traps never catch junk.

💰 Treasure

Roughly 1 in 5000 catches pulls up a container full of loot. Real fishing rods only — the improvised rod never rolls — and each haul costs the rod a third of its health. Admins define the containers and contents; every slot rolls separately so no two hauls match. Off with a single toggle.

📊 Admin Logging

Timestamped session logs in $profile:Gebs/logs/. Debug level 1 gives per-cast summaries; level 2 adds per-fish tables (who is in the pool, abundance, weather and temperature multipliers, bite speed) that answer "why isn't fish X spawning."

Preparing Your Catch

Gutting isn't a craft recipe in the usual sense — combine any knife with a fish to fillet it. What you get depends on the species:

  • Standard fish — 1 to 10 fillets, depending on the species (the big sharks give the most)
  • Roe-bearing fish (trout, salmon, sturgeon, pike, muskellunge) — fillets plus a caviar, subject to the configurable caviar chance
  • Lobsters — a tail plus claws
  • Crabs — legs

The gebsfish fish knives (eleven colours) work like any knife but fillet faster, by a server-configurable multiplier.

18 species can't be gutted at all — the crayfish, minnows, frogs, salamanders, clams, mussels, snails, starfish and jellyfish, plus sardines, shrimp and bitterlings. They're eaten whole instead, and the minnow, salamander, shrimp, bitterlings and sardines double as hook bait. Frozen fish can't be filleted either — let a cooler-chilled catch thaw first.

Finding Bait

Two gathering actions, both configurable and both able to come up empty:

  • Dig for worms — use a digging tool on suitable ground. Can also turn up a Grub Worm
  • Dig for bugs — same idea, done crouched with the Bug Catcher, and yields insects and worms, which go straight into the catcher (onto the spot you dug if it's full)

Both wear the tool on every completed dig, whether or not you find anything, and digging for bugs also trains soft skills. Digging worms rolls the find chance once for each worm the tool can turn up; digging bugs rolls it once per dig.

Full Guide

Everything the mod does, how it fits together, and every gotcha worth knowing before it surprises you. If you only read one part, read Caveats & Gotchas at the bottom. If you want to know exactly why you caught what you caught, read The Odds, In Detail.

1. Getting Started

Gebsfish needs Community Framework (CF) loaded alongside it, on both the server and the client. Nothing else is required.

To catch your first fish you need a rod with a hook. Assemble the rod, attach a hook, then face water and use the rod. Bait on the hook makes fish bite sooner and decides which fish you're likely to get. Traps and nets are separate paths that need no rod at all.

Vanilla DayZ fishing still works exactly as it did. Gebsfish replaces what comes out of the water and adds systems around it — it does not change how you cast.

2. Catching Fish

The five methods

  • Rod — the main method. Works in ponds, lakes and sea. Every species with rod in its catch methods is available to you, including minnows, frogs and salamanders.
  • Large trap — passive. Set it and come back. Catches bigger species that rods also see, plus small things rods never catch: sardines, bitterlings, shrimp, mussels, snails, starfish, jellyfish, crabs and lobsters.
  • Small trap — passive. Catches the small aquatic life, and it's the only way to get crayfish and blood clams.
  • Bamboo net — its own catch table, separate from the species list, configured under BambooFishingNetSettings.
  • Spear — stab into shallow water with an Improvised Spear (bone- or stone-tipped). Also its own catch table, under SpearFishingSettings.

What actually decides what bites

Every cast builds a weighted pool from the 80 species. A fish has to clear three gates before it can even enter that pool:

  1. Environment — 1 pond/freshwater, 2 sea/saltwater, 3 both. A sea fish will never appear in a pond.
  2. Catch method — a bitmask you add together: 1 rod + 2 large trap + 4 small trap. So 3 = rod and large trap, 7 = everything.
  3. Catch probability — a 0–25 abundance weight. 0 means uncatchable, which is how you retire a species without deleting it.

Once a fish is in the pool, these multiply its odds:

  • Weather — per-species rain and storm multipliers. Snowfall counts as rain, so Sakhal, which snows instead of raining, gets them too.
  • Time of day — separate dawn / day / dusk / night multipliers.
  • Water temperature — each species has an optimal temperature and a survivable range. Outside that range it drops to a tenth of its normal odds — heavily suppressed, but never quite zero.
  • Bait — roughly 870 seeded bait/lure-to-fish pairings bias which fish takes the hook.

Bite speed is separate from what you catch — it controls how long you wait. Each species carries a 24-hour activity curve, and the active pool's aggregate sets the delay between bites. Night fishing for catfish genuinely feels different from midday panfishing. The moon phase works on this side too: real synodic math from the in-game date makes full-moon nights bite up to 20% more often and new-moon nights 10% less, without changing which fish it is.

3. The Odds, In Detail

This section is the arithmetic behind every catch — the real formulas, the real percentages, and the reasoning that produces them. Every number here is computed from the shipped default config, so if you have edited fish.json or junk.json your server's numbers will differ. The method is identical either way.

The one thing to understand first

Two completely separate systems are running, and almost every "the multipliers don't work" report comes from conflating them:

QuestionAnswered byUsesRolled
How long until something bites?ModifySignalProbabilityThe global multipliers in WeatherSettings — one set of numbers for the whole serverEvery signal cycle, continuously
What is on the end of the line?GenerateResultThe per-species multipliers in fish.json, plus baitOnce, the moment you cast
The fish is decided when you cast, not when you reel in. The pool is assembled and drawn from at the start of the action, and that result is locked in — the bite timing that follows only determines whether you get it. Nothing you do mid-cast changes which species is waiting.

The weight formula

Every eligible species gets a weight. That weight is what the draw actually uses:

// per species, recomputed every cast speciesMul = min( rainOrStorm × timeOfDay × temperature , MaxStackedMultiplier ) weight = speciesMul × baitMul // the pool: each species contributes its weight once per abundance point fishTotal = Σ CatchProbability(i) × weight(i) junkTotal = fishTotal × JunkShare ÷ (1 − JunkShare) // junk.json, default 0.1 poolTotal = fishTotal + junkTotal P(species) = CatchProbability × weight ÷ poolTotal P(junk) = JunkShare

Four things about this are worth pausing on, because they explain most surprising results:

  • Client and server make the same draw. The roll comes from the random number both sides share, over the same weights, so the fish your screen shows is the one the server spawns. The weights are divided by the largest one before the roll, which changes nothing about the odds.
  • CatchProbability and the multipliers do different jobs. Probability is a whole-number abundance count (0–25); the multipliers are a continuous scale on top. A probability-3 fish with a 2.0× multiplier still loses to a probability-22 fish sitting at 1.0×.
  • Bait is applied after the cap. MaxStackedMultiplier clamps the weather × time × temperature product, then bait multiplies on top of the clamped value. With the default 2.0 cap and a 2.5× bait, one species can reach 5× — bait is the single strongest lever in the system.
  • Junk is a fixed slice, not a competitor. Its weight is worked out again on every cast so that it comes to JunkShare of the pool, and the fish share the rest between them. Conditions and bait decide which fish you get, never how often junk comes up — see Why you sometimes catch nothing but boots.

Water temperature: the strongest natural filter

Temperature is a piecewise-linear tent per species: 1.0 at TempOptimal, falling away linearly in both directions, and flattening to a floor of 0.1 once you pass TempMin or TempMax.

water ≤ TempMin → 0.1 // MinTempMultiplier water < TempOptimal → lerp( 0.1 → 1.0 ) across TempMin..TempOptimal water = TempOptimal → 1.0 water > TempOptimal → lerp( 1.0 → 0.1 ) across TempOptimal..TempMax water ≥ TempMax → 0.1 // MaxTempMultiplier
Out of range is a 10× penalty, not a ban. The floor is 0.1, not 0 — you can still land a bluegill in near-freezing water, it is just ten times harder. Set MinTempMultiplier / MaxTempMultiplier to 0 yourself if you want a hard seasonal lockout.

Time of day, and the cap that actually bites

The day is cut into four windows. Exactly one applies at any moment — they never overlap or stack with each other.

Rain and storm are mutually exclusive — a storm replaces rain rather than adding to it. Snowfall counts as rain: the heavier of the two picks the band, which is what makes the weather buffs work on Sakhal. What does stack is rain/storm × window × moon, and that product is clamped by MaxStackedMultiplier (default 2.0). Why you sometimes catch nothing but boots

Junk sits in the same pool as the fish, but it is not weighted like one. No weather, time, temperature or bait multiplier touches it. Instead, on every cast, junk's weight is set from the fish that can bite there, so that junk always comes to JunkShare of the pool — 10% by default, about one catch in ten, on every map, in every season and at every hour.

fish weight = speciesMul × baitMul // near 0 … 5.0 junk weight = fishTotal × JunkShare ÷ (1 − JunkShare) // all junk entries together, every cast

That is close to vanilla DayZ, which registers five junk items next to its own fish: about 10.6% of rod catches are junk on Chernarus and Livonia, and about 5% in Sakhal's sea. A fixed junk weight would not hold that rate here, because cold water pushes every fish toward a tenth of its weight while junk keeps all of its own: Sakhal's sea would come up junk close to half the time. Tying junk to the fish keeps cold maps at the same one in ten as warm ones.

  • Players: boots are luck, not a sign of bad water. Weather, temperature and bait change which fish takes the hook, never how often junk comes up — the worked example above is 10% junk with or without the worm. Three junk catches in a row is a one-in-a-thousand streak.
  • Admins: JunkShare in junk.json sets the rate for every map at once, from 0 (no junk) to 0.9; the Config Editor's Junk tab has a slider for it. Each junk entry's CatchProbability only decides which junk comes up: with the default four wellies and the pot at 5 each, one junk catch in five is the pot.
  • Water with no fish: where no species can bite a rod — every species for that water switched off, or all of them at weight 0 because MinTempMultiplier is set to 0 and the water is too cold for every one — a rod pulls only junk, as vanilla does in Sakhal's lakes.

Treasure, decomposed

A treasure hit is a gate plus three nested random choices. First the rod check, then the trigger roll, then the container, how many slots it has, and each slot independently.

0. Right rod? improvised rod → no roll at all // RequireRealRod, before the chance 1. Does it happen? roll ≤ 0.0002 // once per successful catch 2. Which container? weighted pick from TreasureContainers 3. How many slots? random in that container's MinItems..MaxItems 4. What is in it? per slot, weighted pick from TreasureLoot

The gate runs before the trigger roll, so casting with the crafted improvised rod never even burns a chance — the 1-in-5000 only ticks on a proper rod. And the win is paid for in tackle: every successful pull removes maxHealth ÷ RodCatchesToRuin from the rod — a third of its health at the default 3 — so a pristine rod affords exactly three treasures before it's ruined. Reading the numbers off your own server

You do not have to take all of this on faith. DebugLogs in general.json writes the inputs to $profile:Gebs/logs/ for every cast. Level 1 prints:

  • The cast's conditions — in-game date, hour and rain, and the water temperature with its parts (map, season, offset).
  • The pool — every species eligible for this cast, and which one was picked.
  • The bite-speed aggregate and the fish dominating it, and every clamp the moment it happens, so a multiplier that isn't behaving is visible rather than inferred.

Level 2 adds the tables:

  • The assembled pool, one row per species with its abundance, environment mask and method mask. This answers "why isn't fish X spawning" directly, because a fish absent from the list failed a gate.
  • A per-species bite-speed table with each fish's activity this hour, abundance and combined weather × time × temperature multiplier.
  • The global bite-rate breakdown (rain/storm, window, moon) and the bite chance on every tick.

The bait multiplier and the final per-species weight aren't logged; work them out from bait.json with the formula above. Or skip the arithmetic: the Config Editor's What will bite? tab runs this exact calculation on your own files for any map, date, hour, rain and bait, and shows every species' share, the junk share, the bite timing and the treasure odds.

Level 2 is genuinely verbose — it writes several dozen lines per cast. Use it to answer a specific question and then turn it back down; leave it on and a busy server will produce enormous logs. Logs older than 3 days are pruned automatically, which limits the damage but does not eliminate it.

4. Tools & Gear — what each one is for

The Items & Gear tab lists everything the mod adds. This section is about what each piece actually does for you, and when it's worth the inventory space.

🎣 Fishing rods

Ten coloured rods on top of the vanilla one. They're cosmetic in performance but wear whenever a fish bites — on a catch, and on a bite you let get away. Reeling in when nothing has bitten costs no durability and never the bait, though it can rarely cost you the hook. A rod can be repaired back up to Worn, never to pristine.

🔪 Fish knives

Eleven colours. Any knife fillets a fish, but the gebsfish knives are the ones worth carrying: about 54% more durability than a vanilla hunting knife and 10% faster filleting (server-configurable via FishKnifeSpeedMultiplier). If you fillet in bulk, this is the single biggest quality-of-life item in the mod.

🧰 Fishing Rod Repair Kit

Combine with a damaged rod to restore it, up to Worn. The kit holds 4 repairs and is used up after the last one.

🕸 Bamboo Fishing Net

Craft from a Long Stick + Netting; repair with more Netting. The net is an action you perform at the water's edge, with its own catch table, separate from the 80-species list — it pulls minnows, frogs and salamanders rather than the rod pool. Catches drop into its own 4×4 cargo, falling back to your feet if that fills up.

🔱 Spear Fishing

Either Improvised Spear (bone- or stone-tipped) can fish. Stand in or beside the water, aim at a spot within 3 m where it is at most 1 m deep, and stab: no bait, a short action, and a 4% chance per stab (a fish about every 2½ minutes, a little slower than a rod) to catch something from its own table — carp, bluegill, yellow perch, redbreast sunfish, largemouth bass, bowfin and bullfrogs in fresh water; southern flounder, white grunt and blue tang at sea; flathead mullet and alligator gar in both. The catch lands at your feet, and each stab wears the spear a little. Like a rod, which rolls for a predator once per cast, the spear rolls once per fish it lands. Admins tune it under SpearFishingSettings in general.json (on/off, find chance, maximum depth, predator chance, catch table), or in the Config Editor.

🪤 Traps

Large and small traps are the passive alternative to casting. The small trap catches the small aquatic life and is the only way to get crayfish and blood clams; minnows, frogs and salamanders also take a hook or turn up in the bamboo net. The large trap adds what a rod never catches: sardines, shrimp, mussels, crabs and lobsters. If you want live bait or a Bait Bucket stocked, this is how: the shrimp, sardines and bitterlings the traps bring up all go on the hook.

🧊 Coolers

Eleven colours. A cooler actively chills its cargo toward −5 °C: rot stops, live bait stops dying, and food freezes. It's the only container that preserves everything — but see the caveat below.

A cooler will freeze your catch, and frozen fish can't be filleted. That's the trade-off for perfect preservation — let it thaw before you gut it.

🪣 Bait containers

Three containers, three different jobs, and they do not overlap — see the table in the next section. The short version: the Worm Container and Bug Catcher keep live bait alive, the Bait Bucket keeps aquatic catches from rotting, and tackle boxes preserve nothing at all.

🧰 Tackle boxes

Small, Medium and Large variants across many colours. Pure storage — they hold lures, live bait (shrimp, bitterlings and sardines too), tools and the fish knives, and the Large and Medium boxes take vanilla knives too. They preserve nothing, which is exactly what makes the dedicated containers worth carrying alongside them. Every tackle box, the small one included, is also deck-mountable on the jon boat.

🏆 Fish mounts

Trophy plaques in three sizes — crafted, wall-mounted, and permanent once a fish is on it. See Trophy Mounts below.

👕 Angler clothing

Fishing hats, shirts, raincoats, wellies and gloves in red, green, blue, purple, orange, yellow, brown, light blue, lime and pink; the gloves are knit work gloves with a black rubber-dipped palm. Cosmetic — they carry no fishing bonus; the raincoats and wellies keep out the rain and cold exactly as vanilla's do, and shirts and raincoats have vanilla's pockets. Every colour is one shade across the mod (the large tackle box's, and the green cooler's for green), so a red hat matches a red rod, knife, cooler and tackle box. Each piece spawns one at a time, so a given colour is rare.

6. Handling Your Catch

Gut a fish with any knife to get fillets. Some species also yield a bonus: caviar from roe-bearing fish, a tail and claws from lobsters. Crabs give legs. Fillets cook like any vanilla meat.

18 species cannot be gutted at all — by design, not a bug. They're eaten whole instead, and the minnow, salamander, shrimp, bitterlings and sardines double as hook bait:
Bitterlings, Sardines, Shrimp, American Bullfrog, Black Devil Snail, Blood Clam, Blue Jellyfish, Cave Crayfish, European Crayfish, Fathead Minnow, Florida Crayfish, Monongahela Crayfish, Mussel, Red Salamander, Red Swamp Crayfish, Rusty Crayfish, Signal Crayfish, Starfish.

Frozen fish cannot be filleted. If you have been carrying a catch in a cooler it will freeze — let it thaw before you try to gut it.

Filleting has two side effects worth knowing: it can draw a predator (see below), and roughly 1 in 250 fillets recovers a damaged hook or lure "stuck in the fish."

Predators

Fishing, filleting, a failed cast, net use and spear fishing can each attract wolves or bears — about 1% per action by default, configurable per action. Spawns are land-only, so no underwater wolves. Everyone nearby may hear a warning sound; the player who caused it also gets a chat warning. At most 30 are out at once, and one still alive after 15 minutes despawns when no player is within 150 m.

7. Treasure

Very occasionally a catch brings up more than the fish: a container full of loot, which lands at your feet.

  • Roughly 1 in 5000 catches by default. That's catches, not casts — failed casts cost you nothing. A session that lands 60 fish has about a 1.2% chance of seeing one
  • Only on a real fishing rod. The crafted improvised rod is a stick and a length of rope — it never even rolls. The vanilla rod and all ten gebsfish colour rods qualify
  • It wrecks your rod. Winching a loaded container up costs the rod a third of its maximum health — three treasures ruin a pristine rod, and one already worn from ordinary fishing gives out sooner. Bring a repair kit or a spare
  • The container appears at your feet, since they're generally too big for a pocket, and a message tells you it happened. Take what you want: a container nobody touches despawns after about two hours
  • Contents differ every time. Each slot in the container rolls the loot pool independently, so two identical chests won't hold the same things

Server owners define both halves in general.json. TreasureContainers lists what the treasure can arrive as — each entry with its own weight, health range and item count, so a rarer container can be made to hold more. TreasureLoot lists what can be inside, each with a weight, health range and quantity range for stackables. Set any entry's weight to 0 to retire it without deleting it.

It ships seeded with plain vanilla classnames so it works immediately, but the pools are meant to be replaced — this is the one feature where the whole point is that you decide what's worth finding.

Turn it off entirely with TreasureSettings.Enable: 0, or by setting Chance to 0. Tune the rarity with Chance — 0.0005 is about 1 in 2000, 0.00005 about 1 in 20000. RequireRealRod: 0 lets the improvised rod find treasure too, and RodCatchesToRuin sets how many pulls ruin a pristine rod (0 = no rod wear).

For the full decomposition — the container odds, expected item counts, per-item chances and how long a first find actually takes — see Treasure, decomposed.

8. Trophy Mounts

The fish mounts are oval plaques of edge-glued planks with a brass nameplate. Hang one on a wall and display a real catch on it.

  • Three sizes. The board goes by the size of the catch's model. The Small Fish Mount takes every bass and trout, perch and panfish, walleye and sauger, bowfin, chinook and cherry salmon, the Pacific bonito, yellowfin tuna and yellowtail snapper, mullet, rockfish, sculpin and minnows, vanilla's carp, mackerel, walleye pollock and steelhead trout, and the crayfish, lobsters, shrimp, shellfish, starfish, jellyfish, frogs and salamanders. The Medium Fish Mount takes pike and the muskies, flathead catfish, alligator gar, northern snakehead, Pacific cod, great barracuda, sockeye salmon, southern flounder, largehead hairtail, the king and snow crab, and the reef and tropical fish (angelfish, blue tang, severum, redhead cichlid, Siamese tigerfish, humphead wrasse, white grunt). The Large Fish Mount takes the lake sturgeon, blue marlin, sailfish, mahi-mahi and the sharks (great white, hammerhead, leopard and angel shark). Any board also takes smaller catches; one that's too big is refused. A trophy hung on the 3.3.2 Wooden Fish Mount (now the small plaque) that is too big for it can be taken off and moved to a bigger board.
  • Every catch hangs like a real mount — upright and side-on, good side out, head to the right, centred above the nameplate. Big fish overhang their board the way big-game mounts do.
  • Craft it from Planks + 1 Metal Wire, with a Hacksaw on you: 1 plank for the small mount, 3 for the medium, 6 for the large. Combine the planks and wire and pick the size; the saw is not consumed but does take durability, and the craft is refused without one.
  • The large board is a heavy item: it doesn't fit in a backpack and is carried in both hands, like a sea chest.
  • Place it by holding to place, then aiming at a wall within about 2.5 m of your feet — it snaps flat and hangs face-out like a picture. Out of reach, the hologram turns red. Aim somewhere without a wall and it stands upright on the floor instead.
  • Then mount the fish: drag it onto the board's slot in the vicinity panel. A board in your hands or pack takes no fish, so picking a fish up never mounts it by accident.
  • The trophy is the actual fish you caught, not a generic model. Its weight and quality persist, so a personal best stays a personal best.
  • Mounted fish never rot. Decay is paused entirely while the fish is on the plaque.
Mounting is permanent. Once a fish is on the plaque you cannot detach it, drag it out, or take it to hands. Destroying the mount does not give the fish back: the trophy is destroyed along with the plaque. Mounting a catch is a one-way decision — otherwise the plaque would be a free never-rots fish locker.

Ruined and rotten fish are refused up front. And note the two separate timers: the fish never rots, but the board itself lasts 45 days untouched, the same as a tent or barrel.

9. The Jon Boat

A drivable flat-bottomed boat in five variants — green aluminum, gray aluminum, and desert, snow and forest camo. It has its own damage zones (chassis, engine, three floaters), a spark plug slot, and cargo space.

Deck mounts: two deck slots each take a cooler or a tackle box and show it sitting on the deck. Both slots accept the same families, so run two coolers, two boxes, or one of each.

Where it spawns: at the map's own boat spawn points, next to the vanilla boats, once the server registers gebsfish-events.xml (see Economy files in the Server Admin Guide). There's at most one of each colour, and the vanilla boats keep every spawn they had.

The vanilla jerry can is deliberately not deck-mountable. A deck slot gives one fixed position and rotation to everything attached to it, and the jerry can's model axes do not match the gebsfish containers — no single orientation suits both. More boat content is coming in a future update.

10. Server Admin Guide

The four config files

Everything lives in $profile:Gebs/. Each file documents its own fields in built-in ...Info strings, so the file is its own reference:

  • general.json — global settings, weather, moon, temperature, predators, recipe toggles, hook recovery, per-action net/dig tables
  • fish.json — all 80 species, one row each
  • bait.json — the bait/lure preference matrix
  • junk.json — junk catch tables

How config updates work

This decides which of your edits survive a mod update, so it is worth understanding once:

  • Each file carries its own ConfigVersion. A file is only rewritten when something actually changed — a fresh generation, a re-seeded section, or a version bump. An up-to-date file is left completely alone.
  • A version bump rewrites all four files, even if only one gained anything. Your values are carried through untouched: the file is re-serialized from what was loaded, not regenerated from defaults.
  • Your values are never overwritten. Updates are strictly additive — missing default entries get inserted, existing entries are left exactly as you set them.
  • Deleting an entry does not disable it. A row you remove counts as missing and gets re-added on the next version bump. Set its weight / chance / CatchProbability to 0 instead — that survives every update.
  • Three lists refill when emptied: HookFromFishCatches, TreasureContainers and TreasureLoot come back with the defaults if you clear them. Set their weights to 0 instead (or switch the feature off).

Economy files

On server start the mod writes gebsfish-types.xml and gebsfish-spawnabletypes.xml into $profile:Gebs/mpmissions/. You must merge these into your mission's economy files yourself — the mod cannot do it for you. They contain only Gebsfish classnames; vanilla fish are already in your mission's own types.xml.

A third file, gebsfish-events.xml, puts the jon boats at the map's boat spawn points, next to the vanilla boats. Copy it into a gebsfish folder in your mission folder and register it in cfgeconomycore.xml with <ce folder="gebsfish"><file name="gebsfish-events.xml" type="events"/></ce> (the file's header shows the same lines). The economy reads it after db/events.xml and only adds to the map's own VehicleBoat event: the five jon boats join the vanilla boats, one of each colour at most, and the event's nominal and max go up by five, so the vanilla boats keep every spawn they had. The vanilla boat types, positions and other settings stay as they are. The file repeats the event's own flags line from db/events.xml: the economy clears an event's flags when a later file leaves them out, which would stop wrecked boats being cleaned up. The mod rewrites the file at every start from your mission's db/events.xml, so if you change the boat event there, copy the file again after the next start. gebsfish-types.xml has to be in your economy too, or the economy leaves the jon boats out. If you'd rather not register a file, paste its five <child> lines into the VehicleBoat event in db/events.xml and add five to that event's nominal and max.

Logging and debugging

Timestamped session logs go to $profile:Gebs/logs/. DebugLogs: 1 gives per-cast summaries; DebugLogs: 2 adds per-fish tables (pool membership, abundance, weather and temperature multipliers, bite speed) that answer "why isn't fish X spawning." See Reading the numbers off your own server. Logs older than 3 days are deleted automatically at startup so an unattended server does not accumulate one file per restart.

11. Caveats & Gotchas

Everything below is intended behaviour that commonly reads as a bug.

For players

Some things can't be gutted

18 species are catch-only — all the crayfish, minnows, frogs, salamanders, clams, mussels, snails, starfish and jellyfish, plus sardines, shrimp and bitterlings. They're eaten whole instead.

Frozen fish won't fillet

Cooler-chilled fish freeze. Let it thaw first.

Tackle boxes preserve nothing

They carry bait but never keep it alive. That is what makes the dedicated containers worth the space.

The Bait Bucket won't take worms

It keeps small aquatic catches (and the minnow, bitterling, sardine, salamander and shrimp baits) from rotting, but refuses worms and insects outright.

Mounting a fish is permanent

No detaching, ever — and destroying the mount destroys the fish with it.

The plaque still expires

The mounted fish never rots, but the mount itself has a 45-day untouched lifetime like a tent.

Fishing can get you killed

Casting, filleting, failed casts, nets and spears can all draw wolves or bears.

Deck slots are picky

Jon boat deck mounts take coolers and tackle boxes only — not jerry cans.

For server admins

Never delete config rows to disable something. Deleted entries are treated as missing and get re-added on the next version bump. Set weight, chance or CatchProbability to 0 instead.
BiteSpeed must be a space-separated string of 24 hourly values ("0.85 0.9 1 …"), not a JSON array: the mod reads the field as text, and a list in its place doesn't load.
Keep every config string under about 900 characters. The engine's reader hard-crashes past roughly 1024, and the limit applies to the built-in default text too, not just your edits.
  • CF is required on both sides. Server-only installs will not work.
  • A version bump rewrites all four config files, not just the one that changed. Values are preserved — the timestamps changing is expected, not data loss.
  • Economy XML is not merged for you. The generated types must be copied into your mission or nothing spawns.
  • There is no migration from fishingsettings.json. Upgrading from pre-3.3 archives the old layout into $profile:Gebs/gebs_oldfiles/ for reference and generates fresh defaults — re-apply your tuning by hand.
  • Fish quality defaults to 1.0 deliberately, because DayZ-Expansion-Market, TraderPlus and Dr. Jones only accept full items. FishQuality is how full each caught fish comes out (0–1), and with it how much the fish weighs; values above 1.0 act exactly like 1.0. Lower it only if your trader buys partly-full items.
  • Logs older than 3 days are deleted at startup. Archive anything you want to keep.
  • Disabled systems keep their tuned values in JSON with no in-game effect, so toggling one off never loses your numbers.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do I need to run Gebsfish?
Gebsfish plus Community Framework (CF), loaded on both server and client. That's it — everything else is optional tuning.
Where are the config files?
In your server profile folder: $profile:Gebs/general.json (global settings, weather, predators), fish.json (all 80 species), bait.json (the bait preference matrix), and junk.json (junk catches). Delete any of them and the server regenerates fresh defaults on next start. Every file documents its own fields in built-in ...Info strings.
I'm upgrading from an older version — where did fishingsettings.json go?
The old single-file config was replaced by the four files above. It's a clean break: there is no automatic migration, so the server generates fresh defaults and you re-apply your custom tuning by hand. Keep a backup of your old file for reference.
Why isn't fish X spawning?
Three usual suspects: its Environment doesn't match where you're fishing (1 = pond, 2 = sea, 3 = both), its CatchMethod doesn't include your method (1 = rod, 2 = large trap, 4 = small trap, added together), or its CatchProbability is 0. Set DebugLogs: 2 in general.json and the server log will print the pool for every cast, with each fish's abundance and environment and method masks; a fish missing from it failed one of those gates.
What are the actual odds of catching a specific fish?
In fresh water with a rod, the pool is 40 species with a combined weight of 408, and junk takes a fixed 10% of catches — so a single fish sits somewhere between 0.7% and 5.5% per catch at baseline. Salt water has a smaller pool (29 species, weight 282), which makes every individual sea species proportionally more likely.

Those baseline numbers then move a lot: weather, time of day, water temperature and bait all multiply a species' weight, and a well-matched bait on a good day can take one fish from 5% to over 17%. The Odds, In Detail in the Full Guide has every formula, the real percentages and a full worked example with charts, and the Config Editor's What will bite? tab computes the odds for your own config.
Why am I only catching boots?
Junk is a fixed share of rod catches — 10% by default, about one in ten, on every map, in every season and at every hour (the server sets it with JunkShare in junk.json). Weather, water temperature and bait change which fish bites, never how often junk comes up, so a run of boots is just luck: three in a row is a one-in-a-thousand streak.

If a rod pulls nothing but junk, no fish can bite in that water: the server has switched off every species for it, or set the temperature penalty to a hard lockout in water too cold for all of them. Vanilla DayZ does the same in Sakhal's lakes. See Why you sometimes catch nothing but boots for the details.
Can I turn off the weather / moon / temperature / bait systems?
Yes, each independently. WeatherCatchBoostEnable, MoonPhaseEnable, TemperatureEffectEnable, and BiteSpeedEnable live in general.json under WeatherSettings; the bait system's master toggle is Enable at the top of bait.json. Your tuned values stay in the file while a system is off.
Why is BiteSpeed a string and not a list?
The mod reads BiteSpeed as one space-separated string of 24 hourly values ("0.85 0.9 1 ..."), so keep that format when editing: a JSON list in its place doesn't load. Text values in your files can be any length, but anything past 1023 characters is cut off when the file loads.
Do the fish work with trader mods?
Yes. Fish quality defaults to 1.0 (a full fish) specifically because popular traders (DayZ-Expansion-Market, TraderPlus, Dr. Jones) only accept full items. If your trader buys partly-full items, you can lower FishQuality in general.json; raising it above 1.0 does nothing.
How do I add the fish to my server's economy?
The mod generates gebsfish-types.xml and gebsfish-spawnabletypes.xml into $profile:Gebs/mpmissions/ on server start — merge those into your mission's economy files. The generated types contain only Gebsfish classnames; vanilla fish are already in your mission's own types.xml. For jon boats at the map's boat spawn points, register the third file, gebsfish-events.xml, in cfgeconomycore.xml (see Economy files); the vanilla boats keep spawning as before.
What is the treasure catch, and how rare is it really?
A very rare chance on a successful catch to pull up a container full of loot as well as the fish; the container lands at your feet. Default is 0.0002 — about 1 in 5000 catches. Note that is catches, not casts, so failed casts cost you nothing: a session that lands 60 fish has roughly a 1.2% chance of seeing one, and a few hundred fish still very probably turns up nothing. That is deliberate — it is meant to be a story someone tells, not something you can farm.

Two catches: it only bites on a real fishing rod — the crafted improvised rod never rolls — and winching a loaded container up costs the rod a third of its maximum health, so three treasures ruin a pristine rod. Admins can relax both (RequireRealRod, RodCatchesToRuin).

The container appears at your feet (they are usually too big to pocket) and its contents are rolled fresh every time, so two identical chests will not hold the same things.
How do I change what treasure contains, or turn it off?
Two pools in general.json, both entirely yours to define:

TreasureContainers — what the treasure arrives as. Each entry has a Weight, a health range, and its own MinItems/MaxItems, so a rarer container can be made to hold more.
TreasureLoot — what can be inside. Each entry has a Weight, a health range, and a MinQuantity/MaxQuantity for stackables like ammo or nails.

Every item slot rolls the loot pool independently. Any classname on your server works, including ones from other mods. Set an entry's Weight to 0 to retire it rather than deleting it — deleted rows come back on the next version bump.

It ships seeded with plain vanilla items so it works immediately; those are placeholders and meant to be replaced. To switch it off entirely set TreasureSettings.Enable to 0 (or Chance to 0). To retune, 0.0005 is about 1 in 2000 and 0.00005 about 1 in 20000.
How do I keep my bait alive?
Live bait (worms, crickets, grasshoppers, grubs) dies about 90 minutes after you find it. Store it in a Worm Container, Bug Catcher or a cooler to pause that clock. The rubber worm is artificial and never dies.

The Bait Bucket is a different job: it holds small aquatic catches — minnows, bitterlings, sardines, crayfish, shrimp, frogs, salamanders, clams, mussels, snails, starfish and jellyfish — and stops them rotting, which also keeps the minnow, bitterling, sardine, salamander and shrimp baits fresh. It will not accept worms or insects. Tackle boxes can carry bait but preserve nothing, which is what makes the dedicated containers worth the space.
Why did my rod break? Can I repair it?
Rods and hooks wear a little whenever a fish bites — on a catch, or a bite you let get away. Repair rods with the Fishing Rod Repair Kit and the bamboo net with Netting, but repairs cap at Worn and Ruined tools cannot be repaired, so retire gear before it's too late.
Can I turn predators off (or up)?
Fully configurable in general.json. PredatorSettings.PredatorSpawnEnabled turns them off everywhere; otherwise each action has its own chance (default ~1% each): fishing, filleting and failed casts under PredatorSettings, net use under BambooFishingNetSettings and spear fishing under SpearFishingSettings. Also there: the predator list with weights and counts, spawn radius, the warning sound, and the chat warning. Set a chance to 0 to turn off that action's predators, or crank them for a high-stakes server (one spawn brings at most 10, and at most 30 spawned predators are alive at once).
Does Gebsfish work on custom maps?
Yes. Chernarus, Livonia, Sakhal, Banov, Namalsk, Deer Isle, Lux, NavalPlus and Deadfall are supported out of the box, and other custom maps work too: if a map's world data replaces the game's list of catches, the mod puts its own catches back once the map has set up, with the map's extra catches kept alongside. Where both define the same catch, fish.json and junk.json decide. Water temperature comes from the map's own world data; a map that doesn't define one uses Chernarus's, so on a colder or warmer custom map set WaterTempOffset to match. If your map's fishing doesn't work, open a ticket in the Discord and we'll issue a hotfix for that map.
Can I put Gebsfish in my server pack / mod pack?
No. Repacking is not permitted under any circumstances — use a Steam Collection to include the mod on your server. See the Usage & Terms.
Can I monetize my server while running Gebsfish?
Yes, provided you follow the DayZ Server Monetization agreement and have permission from Bohemia.

New Systems

  • Weather catch buffs — global rain / storm / time-of-day multipliers in WeatherSettings, plus per-fish Rain/Storm/Dawn/Day/Dusk/Night multipliers on every species (trout favour rain, walleye / pike / catfish favour night, sturgeon favours storms). A stacked-multiplier cap prevents storm + night from compounding past 2.0x. Snowfall counts as rain (the heavier of the two picks the band), so the buffs also work on Sakhal, which snows instead of raining. Fishing rod description hints at the mechanic
  • Moon phase — accurate synodic phase (Meeus Julian Date algorithm) computed from the in-game date drives a night-only catch buff: full-moon nights up to +20%, new-moon nights down to -10%. Independently toggleable
  • Water temperature — per-fish TempOptimal / TempMin / TempMax (degrees C) form a bell curve seeded from ecological buckets (cold deepwater, cold, cool, warm, tropical). The water temperature is the map's own from the game's world data (Chernarus 15 °C fresh / 23 °C sea, Livonia 20 / 25, Sakhal 2 / -0.5) with a seasonal swing (±6 °C fresh, ±3 °C sea, warmest in early August), so Sakhal fishes like an arctic map out of the box; a global WaterTempOffset shifts the whole curve for custom maps or themed servers (frozen lake -10, tropical +5) without editing every fish. If you followed the earlier advice to set WaterTempOffset to -5 for Sakhal, set it back to 0: the map's own cold water is now built in, and the old offset would push Sakhal 5 °C colder still
  • Bite-speed cycle scaling — every fish carries a 24-hour BiteSpeed curve tuned to its real-world circadian pattern; the catching system aggregates the active pool (weighted by abundance and time of day) to drive how long you wait between bites
  • Bait / lure preference matrix — every bait and lure carries per-fish multipliers that bias which fish takes the hook: worms and insects favour panfish and trout, soft-plastic worms favour bass, live minnow and salamander favour pike / musky / walleye, live shrimp favours reef / tropical fish, bitterlings favour pike / musky / walleye / bass, sardines favour the big saltwater fish, in-line spinners favour bass / pike / musky, spoons favour trout and salmon, jigs favour bass / walleye. Numbered lure variants share one family entry (geb_SpinnerBait covers geb_SpinnerBait1-4; an exact numbered entry still overrides). Roughly 870 seeded pairings, all overridable in JSON, master Enable toggle at the top of bait.json. Final pick weight = CatchProbability x min(weather x time-of-day x water temperature, MaxStackedMultiplier) x bait
  • Hook-from-fish recovery — filleting a fish has a small configurable chance (default ~1/250) to recover a damaged hook or lure "stuck in the fish", which drops on the ground with the fillets; the hook pool, weights, and health range are admin-tunable
  • Ultra-rare treasure catches — an extremely rare chance on a successful catch to pull up a container full of loot. Two independent admin-defined pools: TreasureContainers decides what the treasure arrives as (weight, health range, and its own item count), TreasureLoot decides what goes inside (weight, health range, quantity range for stackables). Every item slot rolls the loot pool separately, so no two hauls match even from the same container. A gebsfish container listed there (a tackle box, cooler or bucket) only gets loot it would take. Default chance is 0.0002 — roughly 1 in 5000 catches, not casts, so a session landing 60 fish has about a 1.2% chance of seeing one. Treasure only bites on a proper fishing rod — the crafted improvised rod never rolls (RequireRealRod, on by default) — and winching a haul up is brutal on tackle: each pull costs the rod a third of its maximum health, ruining a pristine rod in three treasures (RodCatchesToRuin, 0 disables the wear). Seeded with plain vanilla classnames so it works out of the box, and meant to be replaced. Toggle with TreasureSettings.Enable, or set Chance to 0
  • Spear fishing — hold an Improvised Spear (bone- or stone-tipped) and stab into shallow water (up to 1 m deep, within 3 m): no bait, a quick action, and the fish of the shallows (carp, panfish, bass, bowfin and bullfrogs in fresh water; flounder, grunts and blue tang at sea; mullet and gar in both). A new SpearFishingSettings section in general.json holds an on/off switch, the find chance, the maximum depth, a predator chance and a weighted catch table with water types; the config editor edits it. A stab catches about one time in 25 (a fish every 2½ minutes or so, a little slower than a rod), and each stab wears the spear a little. Like a rod, which rolls for a predator once per cast, the spear rolls once per fish it lands (1% by default)
  • Cooler freezer system — coolers keep contents cold, live bait is perishable, and a frozen fish can't be filleted until it thaws
  • Bait freshness rules — each container has one job, and they do not overlap:
    • Worm Container / Bug Catcher — hold worms, grubs and insects, and pause the ~90 minute live-bait death timer
    • Cooler — pauses both food rot and the live-bait timer for anything inside
    • Bait Bucket — holds small aquatic catches only (minnows, bitterlings, sardines, crayfish, shrimp, frogs, salamanders, clams, mussels, snails, starfish and jellyfish) and stops them rotting, which keeps the minnow, bitterling, sardine, salamander and shrimp hook baits fresh. It does not accept worms or insects, so it is no help with their ~90 minute clock
    • Water barrel — a vanilla barrel at least half full of water (fresh, clean or salt) is a live well: whole fish and minnows sitting in it don't rot. It doesn't keep worms or insects alive
    • Tackle boxes — can carry bait for convenience but preserve nothing
    • A ruined container preserves nothing: a ruined cooler stops chilling, and a ruined Worm Container, Bug Catcher, Bait Bucket or water barrel stops keeping anything fresh
    • Artificial baits never spoil - they have no freshness timer. The rubber worm sits in the bait slot, so a bite takes it like any bait; the hard lures (spinners, spoons, cranks, jigs) are hooks: they take hook wear on every catch and can be lost like any hook
  • Predator spawn rework — three independent gates (per-action chance, weighted predator pick, per-predator min/max count) with separate chance values for fishing, filleting, failed casts, and net use. Land-only spawn search (no underwater wolves), optional warning sound RPC, configurable chat warning
  • Per-action config sections — BambooFishingNetSettings, DigBugsSettings, DigWormsSettings, each owning its FindChance (per-attempt probability of finding anything) and a weighted Catches[] table. Tool wear still applies on a miss
  • Configurable net catches — net spawn table entries carry an Environment field (1 pond, 2 sea, 3 both) for per-environment filtering; catches spawn into the net's cargo (4x4) first, falling back to the player's feet when full. New bamboo net repair recipe: one Netting + a damaged net restores it to Worn

Config Overhaul

  • fishingsettings.json replaced by four self-documenting files in $profile:Gebs/: general.json, bait.json, junk.json, fish.json. Clean break — no migration: servers regenerate fresh defaults and re-apply their custom tuning by hand
  • Fish are fully data-driven: one Species row per fish (classname, recipe shape, fillet/caviar/lobster results, meat counts, environment, catch method, catch probability, weather multipliers, temperature preference, BiteSpeed curve) feeds a single generic yield + fillet-recipe pipeline
  • Master enable toggles for every catch-modifying system — disabled systems keep their tuned JSON values with no in-game effect
  • Bait multipliers are written rounded to the nearest 0.01, so the file shows clean values instead of float noise
  • Missing config sections re-seed with fully populated defaults; all four files carry a ConfigVersion stamp for future migrations
  • Detailed admin-facing documentation strings on every field, kept under the engine's JSON string-length crash ceiling
  • How the config updates itself — worth reading once before you tune anything, because it decides which of your edits survive a mod update:
    • Each of the four files carries its own ConfigVersion. On startup a file is only rewritten if something actually changed: a fresh generation, a section that had to be re-seeded, or a version bump. An up-to-date file is left completely alone — no rewrite, no modified timestamp
    • A version bump rewrites all four files, not just the one that changed. The stamp is per-file and every file compares it, so bumping the mod version touches general, bait, junk and fish even when only one of them gained anything. Your values are carried through untouched — the file is re-serialized from what was loaded, not regenerated from defaults
    • Your existing values are never overwritten. The update is strictly additive: default entries missing from a list get inserted, entries already there are left exactly as you set them. The one exception is the help text: every ...Info line is documentation, never a setting, so each start puts the current text in (in general.json, bait.json, fish.json and junk.json), and an older file no longer keeps blank or outdated explanations; a file is only rewritten when a text actually changed
    • Deleting an entry is not how you disable it. A row you remove is treated as missing and gets re-added on the next version bump. To retire something permanently set its weight / chance / CatchProbability to 0 — that survives every update. A whole section emptied on purpose is respected, except HookFromFishCatches, TreasureContainers and TreasureLoot, which refill with the defaults when empty (set their weights to 0 instead); a section or list that is entirely absent from the file (or null) gets re-seeded, and so does a net, spear or dig section's missing Catches table
    • New on/off toggles are re-applied during migration. The engine's JSON loader builds each section of your file without its built-in defaults, so a field your file doesn't mention loads as 0, and a toggle introduced in a later version would otherwise arrive switched off on every existing server. On startup the mod checks whether your file contains each newer toggle (CraftFishMount, RequireRealRod, RodCatchesToRuin): a missing one gets its intended default, and once it is in your file the value you set, including 0, is kept through every future update
    • Pre-3.3 layout files (fishingsettings.json, Fish/Logs/, extras/mpmissions/) are swept into $profile:Gebs/gebs_oldfiles/ on first start so old and new never sit mixed together, and the emptied folders are removed afterwards. Nothing from the old monolithic config is migrated into the new files — it is archived so you can still read your old tuning
  • Rows the server can't use are named at startup. A catch row added by hand to the net or spear table without a valid Environment (1 pond, 2 sea, 3 both), a treasure container with MaxItems 0, health levels out of order, a predator with MaxCount 0, and a bait row or preference with no classname each get a log line with the file, the row number, the classname and what the row will do. Nothing is changed in your files. A skipped fish.json species is now named as it sits in the file, with its classname and the reason (the row number used to drift after the first skip)
  • Config editor: a row it can't draw (an empty row, or a fish, bait or preference without a classname) no longer blanks the tab; it's listed with a Remove button and kept in the file, and anything else that fails to draw says so instead of leaving a blank page. Classnames the mod doesn't ship, like the renamed geb_Bonita, are flagged, and What will bite? handles them as the server does. The fish weather, temperature, catch-chance and bite-rhythm fields, the bait multipliers and every table have help buttons
  • Config editor — docs/config-editor.html (the wiki's 🛠️ Config Editor link) opens your four JSON files in the browser, edits them with sliders, toggles and tables, and downloads them again; nothing is uploaded anywhere. Its What will bite? tab shows the odds of every species for a map, date, hour, rain, water and bait, computed from your own files with the same maths the server runs, plus the junk share, the bite timing, the moon and the treasure odds
